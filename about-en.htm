This is the story behind Smartorgan — how I started building organs, why I stopped, what I did in between, and why I'm back.

The beginning

I have always been fascinated by the pipe organ — its complexity, its sound, the way it fills a space. As a young musician and later as an engineer, I felt that there was room for a different approach to digital organs: something that combined the reliability of embedded systems with the sound quality of real sampled pipes. That idea became the Cecilia project.

The Cecilia years (2013–2021)

Between 2013 and 2021, I designed and produced the Cecilia sound system — a family of embedded organ modules built around ARM processors and custom software running on a read-only embedded Linux system. The goal was to create an instrument that sounded like a real organ, was as reliable as a pipe organ, and required no PC, no monitor, no mouse, no operating system updates.

Over the years I developed several variants: the North German baroque module, the claviorganum, the Semplice romantic, and bare electronic units for other manufacturers. I also released CeciliaVPO, a virtual organ software for Windows. The products found their way to churches, conservatories, and private homes across Europe and beyond. I am grateful to every customer who trusted me with their instrument.

The hard turn

In 2021, I had to make a difficult decision. Several things came together at once:

  • Component shortages. The Cecilia hardware depended on specific electronic components. During the COVID pandemic, these became unavailable or prohibitively expensive, with lead times stretching to a year or more. Manufacturing became unsustainable.
  • Software monetization. CeciliaVPO was a Windows application, and I could not find a suitable business model to fund its continued development in a market dominated by established players.
  • Personal circumstances. Life took me in a different direction, and I needed to step away.

So I closed the organ business. It was not an easy choice, and I know it left some customers wondering what had happened. I want to say plainly: I am sorry for the silence. It was never indifference — it was simply that I could not continue, and I did not know when or if I would return.

The software developer years

I found a new job as a software developer. For several years I worked on commercial software projects, building experience with modern architectures, development practices, and team workflows that I had never been exposed to as a solo hardware-software builder.

In hindsight, this was a gift. I returned to the organ world not as the same person who left it, but as a better engineer — with a deeper understanding of software architecture, system design, and what it takes to build something that lasts.

The return

In 2026, I finished my regular job and decided it was time to come back. The pull of the organ never really went away. I have spent the months since then laying the groundwork for what comes next.

What I have ready today:

  • A complete family of MIDI electronic modules for the organ (page coming soon)
  • Novel organ keyboards (page coming soon)
  • Hauptwerk console production — available now

    • What about Cecilia?

    The Cecilia system is not dead, but it cannot simply continue where it left off in 2021. The hardware and software need a complete architectural overhaul — modern processors, a rebuilt sound engine, updated sample format support, and a sustainable design that is not dependent on scarce components. I am working on it, but it is a substantial project and I will not promise a release date I cannot keep.

    For now, Cecilia hardware modules are not available for purchase. If you already own a Cecilia module, it continues to work as it always has. I am committed to supporting existing customers as the new generation takes shape.

    What's next

    The coming months will see the new product pages go live, followed by the first new-generation modules. I plan to share progress updates here and on the contact page. If you want to stay in touch, reach out — I will be glad to hear from you.
